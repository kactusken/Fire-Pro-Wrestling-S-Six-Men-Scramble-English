The Game

Fire Pro Wrestling S - 6Men Scramble (ファイヤープロレスリングS 6メンスクランブル) is a pro wrestling game by Human, released for the Sega Saturn in Japan in 1996. It was the Fire Pro series' first game on the Saturn, and the first with six-man tag matches: up to six wrestlers in the ring, and up to six players with a multitap. It was released only in Japan.

Like every Fire Pro game, it plays on timing rather than button mashing: when two wrestlers lock up, the one who presses at the right moment gets his move, and big moves only land on an opponent who has been worn down first. This guide covers how to play, every mode, the controls and the systems under them, Wrestler Edit, and every wrestler's move list.

  • 160 wrestlers in 14 promotions. Parodies of the real Japanese and American stars of the mid-1990s, plus 24 hidden ones (see Hidden Wrestlers). Every wrestler's moves are in Roster and Move Lists.
  • Victory Road. A story mode: take one wrestler (or a team) through every promotion to unify the HWA titles, with an interview before every match. See Victory Road.
  • Wrestler Edit. Build your own wrestlers from scratch: looks, skills, a full move set and how the CPU fights with them. See Wrestler Edit.
  • Eight match modes, from one-off matches to 32-wrestler leagues, an electrified barbed-wire death match ring and an octagon for no-rules fights. See Modes.

How this guide marks its facts: a rule seen happen in the game is marked verified in game; something read in the game's code or data but not seen running is from the code; advice and numbers from the original Japanese strategy guide are from the guidebook.

Getting Started

When the game starts it asks Use which backup?: System Memory (the Saturn's own memory) or Cartridge RAM (a memory cartridge). The game keeps one save with your records, titles and settings, and a second one for your Edit wrestlers, which needs more room than the Saturn's memory has to spare; see Saving. verified in game

Press Start at the title screen to reach Mode Select. Left and Right pick a mode, A enters it. Most modes then show a Configuration screen (match type, rules, time limit, falls, ring, venue); choose - Next - at the top to go on to Wrestler Select.

On Wrestler Select, L and R page through the promotions, the D-pad moves the cursor, A picks a wrestler and B goes back. The box at the top shows the wrestler under the cursor: Nickname, hometown (From), finisher (Move), height, weight and birth date. The colour bar on the left shows who controls him: 1P to 6P for players, CP for the computer. verified in game

Victory Road

The single-player campaign. Pick a title, a difficulty (Easy, Normal or Hard) and your wrestler, then take on the promotions one by one: beat every wrestler of your class in a promotion to conquer it, and conquer them all to win that HWA unified title. Each promotion's matches follow its own rules (time limits, falls, submissions only in the Fighters' octagon). Before most matches, an interview plays in the ring: rivals trash-talk, partners plan, old men reminisce. verified in game

Some promotions' hidden members challenge you after you conquer their promotion, and beating them unlocks them everywhere: see Hidden Wrestlers.

Exhibition

One match, the mode for playing against friends (ワンナイトマッチ, "One Night Match"). On the Configuration screen you set the Match type (Single, Tag, 6-Man Tag, Mixed), the Rules (2 Count, 3 Count, Sub Only and so on), the Time limit, Falls (1 Fall or 2/3 Falls), the Ring, the Venue and the referee, and who controls each corner (a player or CP). With CP in both corners you can watch the computer wrestle itself. verified in game

Tip

To play a match under a promotion's own rules, choose that promotion's ring and rules here. from the guidebook

Open League

A round-robin league for 4 to 32 wrestlers or teams, players or CPU: everyone meets everyone once. Name your league, set the rules, then enter wrestlers on Wrestler Entry: pick 1P or CP for each entry, and END when done (at least 4). On the league table, move the cursor to a match that hasn't been played and press A; Start opens a menu with Rankings and Save. verified in game

Tournament

A knockout tournament for 4 to 16 wrestlers or teams, entered the same way as the league. Setting the match type to Mixed allows handicap matches such as one against three, and damage carries over from round to round. from the guidebook

Elimination

Team battles with up to five wrestlers a side. Team Edit builds and names your teams (eight save slots); the game also has preset teams such as the factions of its promotions. Three formats: verified in game

  • Elimination: winner stays on; the loser's team sends in its next man.
  • League: every member meets every member of the other team.
  • League (Tug-of-War): a league where the pairings are drawn at random. from the guidebook

If it ends level, the two captains fight to settle it. from the guidebook

Battle Royal

Up to six wrestlers at once, and up to six players. Normal: regular rules, the last one left wins. Endless: wrestlers who lose a fall stay in and keep fighting. Over the Top: going over the top rope to the floor eliminates you. from the guidebook

Death Match

A match or a tournament (Death Match Tourney) in a special ring walled with barbed wire. Its traps: the wire explodes when a wrestler is thrown into it, and the Bomb setting is a timer: when it runs out, the ring explodes. from the guidebook

Gruesome Fight

A match or tournament (Gruesome Fighting Tourney) in an octagon with no ropes, singles only. With no ropes, the Irish whip (the Hammer Throw) only breaks the grapple. The Japanese guide calls it the ring "to decide who's strongest", and the shooters and martial artists are at their best here. from the guidebook

Title Match

Appears on Mode Select once you hold a Victory Road title. It has three entries: verified in game

  • Title Match: defend a belt you hold against a challenger of your choice. The banner after the match is TITLE DEFENDED! (with the defence count) or TITLE WON! (with the new champion's number).
  • Past Champs: each title's history: every champion, the player, the date the title was won and the number of defences.
  • Vacate Title: give up a belt, for example to win it again in Victory Road (the game won't let you challenge for a title you already hold).

Rename and Options

Rename changes the names of promotions and wrestlers (their names, short names and nicknames), with the in-game keyboard. Options: verified in game

  • Screen Zoom: on or off; with it on, the match view zooms in and out.
  • Stereo/Mono, Match BGM Vol. and Theme Vol. (entrance themes).
  • Music Test: listen to the game's music. More tracks appear after the game clear.
  • Fight Records: each wrestler's record: matches, points, total and average match time.

Controls

The Saturn pad: A B C along the bottom, X Y Z above, L and R on the shoulders. In general A is a light move, B a medium one and C (or A+B) a heavy one. The inputs below are from the guidebook; the ones marked otherwise were checked in game.

Footwork matters as much as moves: run to catch an opponent who backs off, circle to get behind him, and back off yourself when you need to. Standing, lean on light and medium strikes: a heavy move such as a dropkick can leave you on the mat too, and is often blocked. Against an opponent who is nearly out on his feet, even a light strike knocks him down.

Tip

Haul a downed opponent to his feet, then hit the heavy move: he's dazed for a moment after he gets up. Watch out for wrestlers who play possum.

Grappling

When two wrestlers meet, they lock up, and the one who presses a button at the right moment gets his move. Winning the timing isn't everything: a move can still fail against an opponent with plenty of stamina left, or be reversed. Start with light moves and work up.

  • Early in a match, heavy moves get reversed. Wear him down with light moves and holds first: the Japanese guide suggests about five light moves before the first medium one. Heavy moves are reversed very often until he's worn out.
  • Front headlock: press Z the moment you lock up, then drag him around with the D-pad; a button takes him down from there.
  • Test of strength: when both wrestlers have about the same stamina and press together, they lock hands. Mash the D-pad to win it (the buttons do nothing), then follow up with a light move.
  • Grabbed from behind: press A (a light reversal) or B (a heavy one) with good timing the moment you're grabbed. Mashing doesn't work.

Running attacks miss unless you're lined up, so set them up with an Irish whip rather than running in blind. Catching the ropes after a whip needs the button the moment you touch them, and stops working once your stamina is low; some fighting styles never manage it (see Fighting Styles).

Tip

After an Irish whip, chase him and hit a running attack from behind before he turns: it's almost never reversed and can knock him out of the ring. Strong enough that you shouldn't overuse it.

Corners and Dives

Whip an opponent diagonally and he crashes into the corner and slumps there. Lock up with him from the front for corner moves (C + direction) or to hang him upside down (Z). These need him worn down first. Some wrestlers have avalanche moves from the top of the post: press C at the moment you lock up there.

From the top of the post, A, B, C or A+B dives; each wrestler's dives differ. Dives connect far more often against a standing opponent: floor him with a big move, wait on the post until he rises (he's woozy getting up), then dive. If he's the one on the post, knock him off with A or B (the Japanese guide also mentions C).

How Matches Work

Fire Pro shows no life bars. Each wrestler has hidden gauges and body parts, and the way he moves (slow to rise, staggering, gasping) tells you how worn down he is. The numbers in this section are from the guidebook unless marked.

Stamina, Spirit, Breath

  • Stamina drops with every move you take, and the lower it is, the more of the opponent's moves land and the fewer of yours do. It slowly recovers while you're not being hit.
  • Spirit keeps you going when the damage peaks: with low spirit you're easier to pin or make submit. Playing to the crowd (L/R) restores it.
  • Breath: every move costs breath; at zero you're out of breath and can't act for a moment. How fast it runs out depends on the fighting style and the kind of move.
  • Neck, arms, waist and legs take damage separately. A worn neck makes you easier to knock out and weakens moves that use the neck; worn arms weaken arm moves; the waist matters for throws; worn legs weaken kicks and dives.

Criticals

A critical knocks the opponent out or makes him give up at once, whatever his stamina. Each wrestler has one critical type, which decides which of his moves can cause one:

So Submission and Tech. wrestlers can win early, and Special and Power wrestlers win late, on a worn-down opponent.

Reversals

Once a wrestler's stamina is low, his opponent's moves are sometimes reversed into his own. What reversals he uses depends on his reversal type (Orthodox wrestlers reverse like any wrestler; Technicians with roll-ups; Power wrestlers with strength; Luchadors with flashy lucha counters; Heels with every illegal trick; Shooters with kicks and joint locks; Grapplers with chokes and holds). Each fighting style is better or worse with each reversal type; the Japanese guide's table gives small bonuses (up to +10) to a style's own type and big penalties to the Giant type for anyone who isn't a giant.

Promotion pressure: wrestling in another promotion's ring can rattle a wrestler, who then starts the match with less stamina and spirit: none, 1/16, 1/8 or 1/4 less. The big promotions (New Japan, All Japan, WCW and Legends) hardly feel it anywhere; wrestlers from the small independents (FMW, WAR, Michinoku Pro, UWF International) and the Shoot Fighters start a quarter down in the rings of New Japan and All Japan.

Moves

Pick an input on the left (Stand A, Grab B↑, Down Head C…) and a move for it on the right. Three ratings at the top help you choose: verified in game

  • Main: the move's main attack type and power, from your Parameters.
  • Sub: its secondary attack type, which counts for less.
  • Affin.: how well the move suits your fighting style. A move that suits the style does far more; a poor fit holds back even a strong wrestler.

Don't take only the strongest moves: build a move set that fits who he is (a striker, a submission technician, a brawler with weapon shots) and it will both play and look right. from the guidebook

CPU Logic

How the computer wrestles him when he's CP, situation by situation (the opponent down by the corner, groggy, whipped into the ropes…), with a percentage for each move in his set. Four overall settings: verified in game

  • Fixation: how much he cares about putting on a show. High goes for the flashy match; low plays it safe.
  • Caution: how carefully he manages the match. High fights not to lose.
  • Flexibility: whether he adapts to each opponent or sticks to his own fight.
  • Teamwork: how well he works with a tag partner.
Tip

Set CPU Logic carefully if the computer will play him, especially as your tag partner, and test it in CPU-vs-CPU matches: a careless setup makes for dull matches.

Saving

Your records and settings need 318 free blocks of backup memory, and the game warns you at start-up if there isn't room. Edit wrestlers have a save of their own: the standard one fits in System Memory, while the extended one, with room for more wrestlers, needs 498 free blocks on a memory cartridge. If a friend saves his wrestlers to your cartridge, your Edit wrestlers can fight his. verified in game

Secrets

Unlockables and hidden content, with how to reach them. The technical side (addresses, how each was found, what was searched for and not found) is in HIDDEN_CONTENT.md.

Hidden Wrestlers

24 of the 160 wrestlers are hidden at first, including a whole hidden promotion. There are no button codes: you unlock them by playing. from the code

Spoiler: how to unlock them

Each hidden wrestler belongs to a promotion. In Victory Road, after you beat every wrestler of a promotion, its hidden member may challenge you, if he's in your class; beat him, or beat him anywhere else, and he's unlocked for good. from the guidebook The game records each one separately when you win against him. from the code

The hidden promotion is Legends, the legends: Rikidozan and three others, "a legend among legends" each. It appears after you conquer every promotion in Victory Road. from the guidebook

Winning both singles titles (Heavy and Jr. Heavy) in Victory Road counts as clearing the game, which unlocks all 24 at once: see The Game Clear. from the code

The Game Clear

Spoiler: what clearing the game unlocks

When both the HWA Unified Heavy Title and the HWA Unified Jr. Heavy Title have champions, the game marks your save as cleared. That unlocks: from the code

  • all 24 hidden wrestlers, with the hidden promotion's page in Wrestler Select;
  • more Music Test tracks (2 to 36 instead of 5 to 34), including the ending and title themes;
  • 200 Edit points instead of 180 (255 with all five titles);
  • in Exhibition, more players than connected controllers.

Endings

Victory Road has two endings, one for the heavyweight titles and one for the juniors, each followed by the staff credits.

Spoiler: the endings' text and pictures

The heavyweight champion walks the corridor to the ring: The fight is not over yet. As long as I am champion, there is no running away... no, as long as I am a wrestler. The door opens on the true Victory Road, and the screen ends on LET'S TRY ENDLESS BATTLE. The junior champion's ending is shorter: One battle is over. But there can be no standing still, for a new battle has begun. The battle goes on, for the title of strongest. verified in game

The Unlocked Patches

The English patch comes in "(Unlocked)" versions (and an untranslated Japanese one) that start the game as if it had been cleared: all hidden wrestlers, the full Music Test and 255 Edit points from the start. They also open three things the original never offers: verified in game

  • Nine hidden Edit presets: Appearance's Face list runs to 212 instead of 203. Faces 204-208 are five complete presets the developers left in (they look like video-game fighters); 209-212 reuse their data.
  • The secret G Gundam theme: the Theme setting in Skill Edit runs to 37, G Gundam Theme, a track the original only plays in its hidden sound test.

Developer Leftovers

Spoiler: the debug menu and other things left on the disc

The disc still holds the developers' tools, named after staff in the credits. None can be reached in the original; the translation project's build tools can switch them on for curious players (build.py --unlocks debug-menu). {V}

  • PROCESS SELECT, a boot menu: HARAKI_PROCESS (a mode selector with a cut "Fight Records" entry), YONE_PROCESS (pad test, backup utility with Saturn floppy drive support, and the stream sound test with the secret G Gundam theme), AIKOH_PROCESS (a wrestler database viewer, test matches and a game-clear switch), KANEKO_PROCESS (the ending viewer) and the GIAN EDITOR, the developers' motion editor.
  • An in-match monitor that prints the wrestlers' positions, damage and current moves over a paused match.
  • Cut moves with names but no animation, among them Ken's Shoryu Reppa from Street Fighter.

Q&A

Is there an English version?

Not from Human: the game was only released in Japan. This guide was written alongside an English fan translation, released as patches for your own copy of the game, with real names or parody names, and with or without everything unlocked.

Why do the names in my game differ from this guide?

The translation comes with the real names of the wrestlers the characters stand for, or with the game's own parody names. Use the Names switch at the top of this page to match your patch.

How do I get the hidden wrestlers?

By playing Victory Road: see Hidden Wrestlers. Or use an "(Unlocked)" patch.

Where is Title Match mode?

It appears on Mode Select once you hold a Victory Road title.

My big moves keep getting reversed.

Heavy moves on a fresh opponent are reversed most of the time. Work him over with light moves and holds first, then medium, then heavy: see Grappling.

My Edit wrestlers won't save.

They need a lot of room, especially the extended save (498 blocks on a memory cartridge). See Saving.
